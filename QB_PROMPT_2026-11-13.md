## Part B. QB study: is there any setting where a quantum model gives a real benefit?

### Background you must use

- Poster (accepted): "Which Features, Which Encoding?" Its contribution is compact feature selection for a fixed quantum feature budget (PCA plus Cohen's d with correlation de-duplication).
- The quantum models do beat plain logistic regression:
  - full data, k=32: VQC π/6 0.951 vs LR 0.945, 5/5 folds; trained scales 0.955;
  - brick-wall n=12 vs LR: +0.005, CI excludes 0;
  - small data: Q3 vs LR +0.010 at N=25 and +0.0045 at N=500 (Holm-adjusted).
- But degree-2 LR (0.958) beats every VQC in 5/5 folds, and a degree-2 function of the angles explains 98.6% of the VQC output. The working hypothesis is therefore: **the win over LR is "the model is mildly non-linear", not "the model is quantum".** Part B tests this hypothesis and looks for a setting that breaks it.
- The π/6 angle kernel is a product of cos² terms with effective dimension about 1.8 and g(RBF‖Q) well below √N. That closes the search for that feature map only. Entangling and re-uploading feature maps have not been screened.

### Pre-registration (write `QB.md` and commit it BEFORE any QB run)

`QB.md` must fix, in advance: the feature maps, the gates, the models, the controls, the tests and the claim wording below. Record every setting tried, including failures, in `results/qb_registry.csv`.

- **Discovery:** outer folds 0, 1, 2 only. **Confirmation:** folds 3, 4 and the external PTB set, used once, at the end.
- State this caveat in `QB.md`: folds 3 and 4 were already used in the main 5-fold studies (the π/6 choice, the selectors), so they are clean only for choices made new in QB. The external PTB set is the stronger confirmation.
- **"Best classical" is always chosen by inner-CV AUC, never by test AUC.**
- **Smallest effect of interest:** +0.005 AUC. A benefit claim needs the lower 95% CI bound of the paired difference above 0 on confirmation, Holm-corrected over the candidates sent (at most 3), and a point estimate of at least +0.005.
- **Dequantized twin rule:** every quantum model is compared with a classical model given the same ingredients. Minimum set: (i) the same bounded inputs a = s·tanh(z) fed to LR, degree-2 LR and SVM-RBF; (ii) a classical kernel with the same frequency content (random Fourier features or a trigonometric polynomial kernel of the same degree). A quantum win that the twin also achieves is reported as "explained by the encoding, not by the circuit".
- **Claim wording, fixed now:** "benefit confirmed" (passes confirmation and beats its twin), "hypothesis for future work" (discovery only), or "no benefit found". No use of the word "advantage" unless a benefit is confirmed against the best classical model and the twin.

### QB1. Training-free screen of new feature maps (seconds per setting)

Reuse `dx_x2_geometry.py`. On 100 and 500 discovery ECGs per fold, first 8 L1 features (and 4), for each feature map compute: g(K_RBF‖K_Q) at λ ∈ {1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-1}, centered kernel-target alignment, effective dimension, mean and variance of the off-diagonal kernel entries, and 5-fold CV AUC of a precomputed-kernel SVM. Also compute the projected quantum kernel (1-qubit reduced density matrices, Huang et al. 2021).

Feature maps (scales s ∈ {π/8, π/6, π/4, π/3, π/2} for each):
- F0: the current product map (reference; must reproduce the X2 numbers).
- F1: re-uploading map. The same 8 features loaded L ∈ {2, 3, 4} times with the fixed CNOT ring between loads (no trained gates).
- F2: IQP / ZZ map. H on all qubits, Rz(a_i), then RZZ(c·a_i·a_j) on ring neighbours, 1 and 2 repetitions, c ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}.
- F3: F2 with RZZ only on clinically paired features (reciprocal leads such as inferior II/III/aVF against I/aVL; neighbouring precordial leads; ST with T of the same lead). Write the pairing table in `QB.md` before running.
- F4: Hamiltonian-evolution map (Heisenberg ring, data as local fields, Trotter steps 1 to 3).
- F5: the brick-wall feature map from `bw_utils.py` (numpy, untrained, n=8), if it runs without torch.

**Gate (fixed now):** a map goes to QB2 only if, at λ ≥ 1e-3 in at least 2 of 3 discovery folds, g ≥ √N / 2 AND the mean off-diagonal kernel value is above 0.05 (not concentrated) AND its centered alignment is at least 90% of the RBF kernel's. At most 4 maps pass, ranked by centered alignment. If none pass, write that result, skip QB2, and go to QB3.

Deliverable: one table (map × scale × N) and one figure of g against √N for all maps. This table is a paper result whichever way it comes out: it extends the scale rule from "which scale" to "which feature map".
